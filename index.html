Setting up the machines

Pick a weight where the last 2 reps feel hard but you can still do them smoothly. If you could do 3+ more, move the pin down one plate next set. Rest 45–60 seconds between sets so your heart rate stays up.

Every brand's stack is different, so treat these as a first guess and adjust on set one. Set the seat so the joint that moves (knee or shoulder) lines up with the machine's pivot point; most machines have a coloured dot showing where.

Progress over 8 weeks

  1. Weeks 1–2: learn the machines. Light weights, 2–3 reps left in the tank. Cardio at the low end of each range.
  2. Weeks 3–4: add weight. When you hit all reps on every set, move the pin down one plate next time.
  3. Weeks 5–8: add a set and time. 4 sets on the first two machines each day. Add 5 minutes to each cardio block.
  4. Week 9: easy week. Half the sets, then start again from your new weights.

Food that matches the plan

The machines help, but most fat loss comes from eating a little less than you burn. Aim for about 1,700–1,800 kcal with protein at every meal. You do not need to cut out carbs.

Simple habits that make it work

  1. Walk 8–10k steps a day. Gym time is a few hours a week; steps add up every day.
  2. Half the plate is vegetables. They fill you up for very few calories.
  3. Drink 2–2.5 L of water. More on gym days. Swap sugary drinks for water or zero-sugar ones.
  4. Sleep 7–9 hours. Short sleep raises hunger the next day.
  5. Weigh in once a week. Same morning each week. Judge the 2–3 week trend; water and your cycle can move one reading by 1–2 kg.
  6. Measure your waist every 2 weeks. It often drops even when the scale stalls.

The calorie and protein numbers assume you are roughly 20–35 years old with a mostly sitting job. If your weight hasn't moved after 3 weeks, take off about 150 kcal a day. If you have a health condition, joint pain, are pregnant, or take medication, check with a doctor before starting. Stop any exercise that causes sharp pain.
